# Website Prompt: A Scroll-Driven Product Site for the Windows Island App

Nov 15, 2026 · @Jeet

Prompt for any coding agent (Antigravity, Claude Code and so on) to build the marketing site for the app (working name \<APP\_NAME>). It is a single-page, scroll-driven site where every feature has a live demo the visitor can use: the island, the Spectrum visualizer, widgets, the optional taskbar, Looks and the settings. It borrows the structure and polish of needlemac.com, but all copy, images, code and layout are original.

## How to use this document

Paste Sections 1 to 12 into the agent in an empty repository. Do phase P0 first and keep docs/MOTION.md up to date as you build. The site demos are built in HTML, CSS and canvas, so they do not depend on the app being finished.

## 1. Goal and success measures

| Goal | How it is measured |
| --- | --- |
| A visitor understands the app in 10 seconds | The hero shows the island working, with the primary button above the fold |
| Show, don’t tell | Every feature section has a working demo the visitor can use |
| Feels premium and smooth | 60 fps scrolling on a mid-range laptop, no jank |
| Fast and private | Lighthouse mobile performance 90 or more, no cookies, no analytics, no third-party requests |
| Converts | A Download button is reachable from the nav at every scroll position |

**Audience:** Windows 10 and 11 users who customise their desktop (gamers, students, creators). **Tone:** calm, short sentences, second person, no superlatives.

## 3. Concept and copy

**Concept:** your music, widgets and system status in one island at the top of your screen. It stays out of the way until you need it.

| Section | Heading (draft) | One line (draft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Your PC, with an island. | Music, widgets and status live at the top of your screen. |
| Island | Hang it from the top edge. | Hover to peek, click to open. |
| Spectrum | Music you can see. | Three visualizers that follow the beat and the album colours. |
| Widgets | Drag it onto your desktop. | Pull any widget out of the island and resize it. |
| Taskbar | Swap the taskbar. Swap it back. | Optional, and Windows’ own taskbar returns exactly as it was. |
| Looks | One PC, four moods. | Pick a look and the whole desktop follows. |
| Settings | Everything has a switch. | Search, tweak, and see it change live. |
| More | Small things, done well. | Clipboard, shelf, reminders, mic mute and more. |
| Free and private | Free. No account. No tracking. | Your data stays on your PC. |

**Truth rule:** a feature may appear on the site only if the app build passes its acceptance list. Anything else carries a ‘Coming soon’ tag. Do not name competitors, do not use the words ‘Dynamic Island’ in the product name, and do not use the Windows logo (say ‘for Windows 10 and 11’ in text).

## 4. Stack and project structure

| Layer | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Build | Vite with TypeScript. Plain DOM and CSS custom properties. No framework is needed. |
| Animation | GSAP 3 (core, ScrollTrigger, Flip, Draggable, SplitText, CustomEase). All GSAP plugins are free, including for commercial use. Confirm the licence text in the installed package. |
| Smooth scroll | Lenis (MIT), driven by the GSAP ticker |
| Visuals | Canvas 2D first for Dot, Bar and Spectrum. Move to WebGL (OGL, MIT) only if profiling shows a need. |
| Audio | Web Audio API: one AudioContext, an AnalyserNode (fftSize 2048) and a procedural demo beat |
| Icons | Fluent UI System Icons (MIT) or Lucide (ISC), as inline SVG |
| Fonts | Self-hosted, subset variable fonts under the OFL. A wide display face (the same one as the app’s day clock), a clean sans for body, a monospace for captions. No Google Fonts requests. |
| Images and video | AVIF and WebP via sharp or squoosh. Short loops with ffmpeg as WebM and MP4. |
| Testing | Playwright for scroll and screenshot tests, Lighthouse CI for budgets |
| Hosting | A static build on Cloudflare Pages, GitHub Pages or Netlify (all have free tiers). A custom domain costs money and is optional. |

Structure: src/sections (one module per scene), src/components (island, spectrum, widgets, taskbar, settings-mock), src/lib (audio, beat, palette, motion tokens, scroll), public/assets.

## 5. Design system

| Token | Value (starting values, tune by eye) |
| --- | --- |
| Base | Background about #070709, text white 92%, secondary text white 60% |
| Surfaces | Glass: white 6% fill, backdrop blur, 1 px white 10% edge, radius 20 to 28 |
| Type | Display clamp(48px, 9vw, 128px) in the wide face. H2 clamp(32px, 5vw, 64px). Body 17 to 18 px. Captions in mono, 12 px, tracking 0.12 em. |
| Spacing | 4 px scale. Sections have 160 px of vertical padding on desktop and 96 px on mobile. |
| Easing | Out-expo cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) for entrances. A custom spring-like ease for toggles. |
| Durations | Micro 0.2 s, UI 0.5 s, scene 1.0 to 1.4 s, stagger 0.06 s, scroll scrub smoothing 0.6 to 1 |
| Grain | A static 3% noise overlay to prevent gradient banding |

**Looks as page themes.** The four Looks (Zen, Golden, Cozy, Glow) are four sets of CSS variables (--bg, --fg, --accent, --clock, --surface). Zen is a light page with a near-black clock; Golden is warm dark with gold; Cozy is deep green; Glow is near-black with pink. Choosing a Look in Section 6.7 re-themes the whole page and is remembered for the session.

## 6. Scenes and scroll choreography

Every scene lists its layout, motion, interaction and fallbacks. Pins use ScrollTrigger. All animation uses transform and opacity only.

### 6.0 Opening sequence

- A black screen. A small pill fades in at the top centre and pulses with a soft ring. A ‘Start the journey’ button and a clearly visible ‘Skip’ sit below it.
- Start: the pill expands into the hero island with a Flip transition (about 1.2 s) while the headline lines rise in. The click also unlocks the AudioContext.
- Skip, a returning visitor in the same session, or reduced motion: go straight to the hero with no animation.

### 6.1 Hero

- Full-viewport desktop scene built in CSS (an original wallpaper, not a Windows screenshot). The island at the top loops through resting, hover-peek and expanded-music states. Weather and PC-stats cards float at different depths.
- Headline reveals line by line with SplitText masks. Wallpaper layers drift slowly. Pointer-follow parallax stays within 12 px.
- Two buttons: Get it (the Store or GitHub link) and Explore (scrolls to the Island scene). A scroll cue fades out after the first scroll.
- Mobile: no pointer parallax. The island loop stays. Reduced motion: a static frame.

### 6.2 Sticky nav

- After the hero a glass pill nav appears at the top centre: the logo, anchor links (Island, Spectrum, Widgets, Taskbar, Looks, Settings, More) and a Download button.
- The active link follows the scroll with a sliding indicator (Flip). A thin progress line runs along the pill’s bottom edge.
- Mobile: collapses to a logo, a menu button and the Download button.

### 6.3 Island

- A pinned scene about 300 vh long. Text steps on the left, a large live island component on the right (real DOM, not a video).
- Scrolling steps the island through resting, hover preview, expanded music, the Widgets tab and Quick controls, with the matching text step highlighted.
- Two toggles under the demo actually restyle it: Shape (Notch or Island) and Surface (Glass, Graphite, Acrylic, Black). The visitor can also hover and click the demo directly.
- Bullets stagger in after the demo settles. Mobile: the pin becomes stacked cards with tap-to-toggle.

### 6.4 Spectrum

- A full-bleed dark scene. A large Spectrum orb (a glowing ring with a dot sphere inside, procedural, original) reacts to the page’s audio. A Play button starts the demo beat. A drop zone lets the visitor load their own audio file, which is decoded locally and never uploaded.
- A style switcher (Spectrum, Dot, Bar) morphs between the three visualizers in 0.4 s. Four sample covers (generated abstract art) change the palette with a 0.7 s cross-fade, to show the colour sync.
- Scroll scrubs the orb from pill size to full size as the scene enters. With no audio playing the orb idles as a calm ring.
- Fallback: no AudioContext or reduced motion shows a static frame and keeps the controls.

### 6.5 Widgets and the day clock

- A desktop mock with the island’s Widgets tab open (calendar, focus timer, weather, PC stats).
- Draggable: the visitor drags a tile out onto the desktop and it pins. The day clock tile grows into the big wide-spaced clock as it leaves the island. A slider and the scroll wheel resize any pinned widget. An Edit layout toggle shows corner handles.
- Visitors who do not interact see a ghost cursor perform the drag once when the scene enters the viewport.
- The focus timer tile and the PC-stats sparklines are live in the demo (the stats are simulated and labelled as a demo).

### 6.6 Taskbar

- A split reveal: a generic taskbar slides down and the glass notch taskbar rises. A Taskbar On/Off switch does it again.
- A small window can be maximised: the notch bar slides away, and pushing the pointer to the bottom edge brings it back, as in the app.
- The caption states that Windows’ own taskbar returns exactly as it was when the option is turned off.

### 6.7 Looks

- A pinned horizontal carousel of four full-viewport desktops: Zen, Golden, Cozy, Glow. Each shows its surface and its day clock colour. Wallpapers are original, generated or public-domain art.
- Choosing a look with the chips re-themes the whole page with a circular wipe that starts at the click, over 0.6 s.
- Mobile: a vertical stack with a sticky chip bar.

### 6.8 Settings tour

- A mock settings window with a working search box that filters about 40 settings live, a sidebar, working toggles and a Corners slider that changes the radius of a live island preview beside it.
- The point is the breadth: every row has a plain-language line under it.

### 6.9 More

- A grid of small cards, each with a hover spotlight that follows the pointer and a slight tilt: Clipboard history, File shelf (drag a file chip into the island), Send to phone (an animated QR code), Mic mute (press M on the page to toggle), Reminders and focus timer (a working 25:00 timer), Downloads (an animated progress), Quick controls (draggable sliders).
- No real camera or microphone access anywhere on the site. The camera card uses an illustration.

### 6.10 Beat Glow

- A line of light runs around the viewport edge in the album colours and follows the beat of the page’s demo audio. It is drawn on one fixed canvas or an SVG stroke, with a toggle in the nav. It is off until the visitor plays audio, and reduced motion keeps it static.

### 6.11 Free and private

- Three statements (Free, No account, No tracking) and a short ‘privacy ledger’ that animates in: what stays on your PC, and the only things that use the network (weather and lyrics, both opt-in).

### 6.12 FAQ

- An accordion with spring height animation. Questions: which players does it work with, which Windows versions, will it slow my PC, what data does it use, is it free, how do I turn off the taskbar feature, what happens in fullscreen games. Answers must match the app’s real limits (for example, exclusive-fullscreen games cannot show the island).

### 6.13 Download and footer

- A large call to action with the Microsoft Store and a GitHub Releases link, system requirements (Windows 10 2004 and later, Windows 11, x64) and a three-step install dialog. Footer: Privacy, Changelog, Credits (open-source licences, data sources), GitHub and Contact.

## 8. Demo audio and the beat engine

- One shared AudioContext, created on the first user gesture. Sound is off by default and always has a visible toggle.
- The demo track is generated in the browser (a kick, bass, hats and a simple chord loop at about 110 BPM), so there is no licensing issue. The visitor can also drop in their own file, processed locally.
- An AnalyserNode (fftSize 2048) feeds a small beat engine: 96 log-spaced bands, attack about 12 ms and release about 160 ms, and spectral-flux beat detection on 40 to 150 Hz with a 150 ms cooldown. This mirrors the app’s own audio engine, so the demos look like the product.
- Every canvas and glow reads the same frame object. Loops pause when the canvas is off screen or the tab is hidden.

## 9. Performance, accessibility, SEO and privacy

- **Budgets:** Lighthouse mobile performance 90 or more, accessibility 95 or more, best practices 100, SEO 100. LCP under 2.5 s, CLS under 0.05, INP under 200 ms. Initial JavaScript at most 250 KB gzipped. Heavy scenes (the orb, the settings mock) load lazily with IntersectionObserver.
- **Smoothness:** animate only transform and opacity, avoid layout thrash, use will-change sparingly, cap devicePixelRatio at 2, and profile the scroll on a mid-range laptop and a mid-range phone.
- **Progressive enhancement:** the base HTML is readable without JavaScript. Animation adds classes on top.
- **Reduced motion:** with prefers-reduced-motion there is no smooth scroll, no pinned scrubbing, no autoplay, and static states for every demo.
- **Accessibility:** semantic landmarks, a skip link, every demo operable by keyboard, focus managed in dialogs, aria-live for demo state changes, text contrast of at least 4.5, decorative video marked aria-hidden.
- **SEO:** title, description, canonical, Open Graph and Twitter cards, JSON-LD SoftwareApplication, sitemap, robots.
- **Privacy:** no cookies, no analytics by default, self-hosted fonts and assets, no third-party requests. A privacy page that matches the app’s.

## 10. Assets and legal

- Do not copy, trace or reuse the reference site’s text, images, code or layout. Take inspiration from the type of experience only.
- Visuals are original: CSS and SVG scenes, generated abstract covers, and wallpapers that are original or public-domain. Real screen captures of the app go in as they become available (list them in docs/ASSETS.md: island hover and expand, Spectrum orb, widget drag-out, taskbar toggle, Look switch, settings search), as short 1280 × 720 loops.
- Product and trademark names appear only as plain text. Use the official Microsoft Store badge once the store listing exists. List every font, icon set and library with its licence on a Credits page.

## 11. Build phases

Do not start a phase until the previous one passes. Report honest pass or fail.

**P0: plan.** docs/PLAN.md, docs/MOTION.md (a table of every animation: trigger, duration, ease, fallback) and the design tokens. *Acceptance:* the motion table covers every scene in Section 6.

**P1: skeleton.** Static, semantic, responsive page with all copy, the nav, Lenis, and no motion. *Acceptance:* readable and usable without JavaScript, and Lighthouse accessibility 95 or more.

**P2: opening and hero.** Sections 6.0 and 6.1. *Acceptance:* Skip works and is remembered, the hero loops at 60 fps, and LCP stays under 2.5 s.

**P3: Island and Spectrum.** Sections 6.3 and 6.4 with the audio and beat engine. *Acceptance:* the toggles restyle the demo, the orb follows the demo beat and a dropped file, and the loops pause off screen.

**P4: Widgets, Taskbar, Looks.** Sections 6.5 to 6.7. *Acceptance:* dragging works with mouse and touch, the Look switch re-themes the whole page without a flash, and the carousel pins cleanly on desktop and stacks on mobile.

**P5: Settings, More, privacy, FAQ, Download.** Sections 6.8 to 6.13. *Acceptance:* search filters live, every card is keyboard-operable, and the download links work.

**P6: polish and deploy.** Performance, accessibility and reduced-motion passes, testing in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (including iOS), then deployment to a free host with a README. *Acceptance:* all Section 9 budgets met, with the numbers recorded in docs/PERF.md.

## 12. Working agreement

1. Write docs/PLAN.md and docs/MOTION.md before building, and update them as the design changes.
2. Be original. Inspiration is allowed; copying the reference is not.
3. Verify library versions and licences when you install them, and record them in the Credits page.
4. Measure, do not claim: Lighthouse and frame-rate numbers go in docs/PERF.md.
5. Respect the truth rule: only advertise what the app really does, and mark everything else ‘Coming soon’.
6. If something here is impractical (for example a scene that cannot hold 60 fps on mobile), say so and propose the closest alternative.
